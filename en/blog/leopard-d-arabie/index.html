Arabian Leopard

The Arabian leopard (Panthera pardus nimr), also known as the Arabian panther, is a subspecies of leopard.

It is recognisable by its coat, ranging from pale yellow to tawny and covered in rosettes. Although it is one of the smallest leopards, it is the largest cat of the Arabian Peninsula.

The rosettes

These are rounded, rose-shaped patterns found on the coats of certain animals, especially felines. They act as camouflage, allowing the predator to blend into its environment.

Way of life

These are solitary animals, except during the mating season or when females are raising their young.

They are territorial, but their territories often overlap without causing any problem. Arabian leopards are mainly crepuscular.

The geographic range of the Arabian leopard is poorly known; it has been observed in the remote, rugged high mountains of the Arabian Peninsula.

The latest estimates suggest that no more than 100 to 120 individuals remain in the Arabian Peninsula, but the data remain very uncertain and the population is declining.

Its diet

The Arabian leopard has a varied diet that includes both small and large prey.

In Oman, they feed on animals such as the Nubian ibex, the Arabian gazelle and the Cape hare, but also on porcupines, rodents, birds and insects.

The rock hyrax (Procavia capensis) seems to be a particularly important food source, especially where large herbivores are scarce.

Threats

Leopards are mainly threatened by habitat destruction, caused by the overexploitation of nature and the expansion of human settlement. This also reduces wild prey populations and increases the risk of conflict with herders, as leopards may be driven to attack livestock.

In Yemen, however, trophy hunting is considered the main cause of leopard killings.

The subspecies is classified as “Critically Endangered” (CR).

A few actions

Oman
1976: hunting, capture and killing banned, followed by a law in 2003.
1997: creation of the Jabal Samhan Nature Reserve and the Arabian Leopard Programme (ALP).
Saudi Arabia
2019: launch of the Arabian Leopard Fund by the Royal Commission for AlUla.
Yemen
2008: the government makes the Arabian leopard its national animal.
2009: creation of the Foundation for the Protection of the Arabian Leopard in Yemen.

In parallel, several captive breeding programmes have been launched to study the animal and work towards its reintroduction into the wild.

Arabian Leopard Day

In February 2022, the Saudi Council of Ministers declared 10 February “Arabian Leopard Day”, in order to protect the species and raise public awareness of its conservation status.

And in June 2023, the United Nations voted to adopt a resolution officially designating 10 February as the International Day of the Arabian Leopard.
